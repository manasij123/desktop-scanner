What it does

Every scanner-app feature you actually use, on the desktop — and tuned against real reference scans, not guessed at.

Finds the page for you

A trained segmentation model plus classic edge detection locate the document even on a cluttered desk or an open notebook — and turn down a photo that has no document in it.

Fixes the angle

Four draggable corners and a perspective warp flatten a page shot from any angle into a straight-on rectangle. Nudge a corner if the automatic guess is slightly off.

Four scan looks

Original, Photo, Docs and Clear — each in colour or black & white. Docs and Clear flatten shadows, neutralise the paper tint, and drive text to crisp black.

Photos and PDFs, in bulk

Drop a stack of images or an existing PDF straight onto the window — or onto the app icon to launch it on them. Set the crop and the look one time; every page in the batch follows.

Reads the text

Built-in OCR extracts selectable text in English and Bengali — copy it straight out, or save it as a .txt file.

One clean PDF out

Reorder, rotate or drop pages, then export a single A4 PDF — or send the whole document straight to a printer.

Reconstruct fine detail

“HD detail” runs a super-resolution model on your device — nothing uploaded — to rebuild crisp edges on text photographed a little too soft or too small to read.

Recover faded text

Where a glare or a bright reflection washed a stroke almost to white, Desktop Scanner re-inks it toward its true darkness — and tells that apart from back-of-page show-through, so bleed-through stays faint.

Fine-tune the result

Layer brightness, contrast and saturation adjustments on top of any look, live — then reset to the preset in one click if you overshoot.

Your documents never leave your computer.

Detection, perspective correction, filtering and OCR all run locally on your machine. There is no sign-in, no cloud step, and no telemetry. Pull the network cable and Desktop Scanner works exactly the same — the model weights and language data ship inside the app.

Three steps

From a folder of phone photos to a finished PDF.

  1. Add your pages

    Drag phone photos or a PDF straight onto the window — or onto the app icon and it opens on them — or use the Add button. One document or a whole stack.

  2. Set the crop and the look

    Confirm the detected corners, choose Clear for a printed-page finish or Photo for a lighter touch, and adjust brightness or contrast if you want to.

  3. Export

    Save one multi-page PDF to your desktop, pull the text out with OCR, or print the document — all offline.
